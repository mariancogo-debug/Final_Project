Our History & Culture

A brief history

Central Luzon has long been the Philippines' rice-growing heartland, and its people include the Kapampangan, Tagalog, Ilocano, and Aeta communities. Pampanga was one of the first provinces organized by the Spanish in Luzon, and the region became a center of trade and farming.

The region played a major role in the fight for independence. In 1897, revolutionaries set up the Republic of Biak-na-Bato in the mountains of Bulacan. In 1899, the First Philippine Republic was inaugurated at Barasoain Church in Malolos, giving the country its first constitution.

In 1942, Bataan was the site of fierce battles in World War II and the start of the Bataan Death March. In 1991, the eruption of Mount Pinatubo reshaped the landscape of Pampanga, Tarlac, and Zambales. Today the region has rebuilt, and the volcano is a popular trekking destination.

Cultural highlights

Festivals

  • Giant Lantern Festival: held each December in San Fernando, Pampanga, with huge, brightly lit lanterns
  • Pulilan Carabao Festival: a May celebration in Bulacan where decorated carabaos kneel before the church
  • Singkaban Festival: Bulacan's cultural festival, held each September

Traditional crafts and foods

  • Parol (lanterns): intricate star lanterns made in Pampanga
  • Buntal hats: hand-woven hats from Baliuag, Bulacan
  • Sisig: a sizzling chopped-pork dish that began in Pampanga
  • Pastillas de leche: soft milk candy wrapped in colorful paper
  • Tibok-tibok: a creamy carabao-milk pudding
